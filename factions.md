**Six boxes** for the XP track. That gives you roughly one advance every five sessions if players are hitting two or three triggers a session, which lands you at two advances over ten sessions — exactly what you want. It also keeps the track visible and meaningful without feeling like a grind.

## THE OPPOSITION

---

### THE FRENCH TEAM — *Les Renards*
*Devil-may-care, politically connected, and utterly unbothered*

The French team operates as though the whole city is their living room and everyone else is a guest who doesn't quite understand the furniture. They have friends in the prefecture, ex-lovers in every arrondissement, and a complete inability to take any of this seriously — which somehow makes them faster. They drink well, argue loudly, and have never once been seen to prepare.

**Tier 2. Hold: Paris (everywhere and nowhere). Reputation: Charming, Unreliable, Dangerous.**

**Disposition toward the English team:** Amused. Faintly contemptuous. Open to flirtation.

**Resources:** Intimate knowledge of every back road in the Île-de-France. Political cover through connections at the Élysée. A mechanic's workshop in Montmartre that doubles as a wine bar.

---

**Lucien Arnaud** — *Languid, Untouchable, Reckless*
Velvet blazer, silk scarf, cigarette permanently in the corner of his mouth. Drives the Alpine A110 like he's bored, which is precisely how he goes so fast. Lucien doesn't compete — he performs. He'll wave at you as he passes. He has opinions about every restaurant in the 6th arrondissement and will share them at length at the worst possible moment. Rumoured to have a brother in the Interior Ministry.

**Jean-Baptiste Marchand** — *Intense, Superstitious, Gifted*
Aviator sunglasses, oil-stained overalls, a rabbit's foot wired to the Citroën's gear stick. Where Lucien coasts, Jean-Baptiste fights. He talks to his DS21 during a race, sometimes argues with it. He is not joking. The car wins those arguments often enough that no one laughs anymore. He has a temper that appears without warning and disappears just as fast.

**Colette Voss** — *Sharp, Watchful, Mercurial*
Half-French, half-Swiss. Slim leather jacket, wide-leg trousers, dark eyes that miss nothing. Officially she is the team's logistics coordinator. In practice she is the person who knows where everything is, who everyone owes, and what is actually going on. She drove rally cars in her early twenties and can still read a road better than most. She didn't stop racing because she wasn't good enough. She stopped for reasons she has never explained.

---

### THE ITALIAN TEAM — *La Scuderia Bella*
*Stylish, competitive, devastating, and fully aware of all three*

The Italians arrived in a matching set of leather luggage and immediately improved the average appearance of every room they entered. They are superb drivers, worse losers, and they treat the whole enterprise as an extension of their personal aesthetic. The cars are immaculate. The outfits are immaculate. Even Beppe, who is always shirtless, somehow looks immaculate.

**Tier 3. Hold: Milan (home base). Reputation: Brilliant, Vain, Formidable.**

**Disposition toward the English team:** Generous condescension shading into genuine respect if earned.

**Resources:** Factory-backed vehicles maintained to a standard that borders on offensive. A suite at the Hôtel de Crillon. More money than anyone else at the table.

---

**"Gianni" Caruso** — *Magnetic, Imperious, Calculating*
Razor-thin moustache, pale linen suit, driving gloves that cost more than a small car. Gianni manages the team the way a conductor manages an orchestra — with absolute authority and complete intolerance for wrong notes. He drives the Ferrari 206 GT with the quiet confidence of a man who has never genuinely doubted himself. He is usually right. This has not made him easier to be around.

**Rafaella "Rafi" Mancini** — *Radiant, Competitive, Distracting*
Red Ferrari 250 GTO, convertible, hair and scarf streaming behind her at speed. Rafi is the best driver on the continent and she knows it. She treats rivals the way a cat treats a piece of string — with intermittent, devastating attention. She will pull alongside you at speed, smile like you're the most interesting thing she's seen all week, and then be gone before you've recovered. She has absolutely weaponised this. She is also entirely genuine, which makes it worse.

**"Beppe" Esposito** — *Meticulous, Inscrutable, Unflappable*
Shirtless, toothpick, enormous wrench, expression of complete serenity. Beppe speaks very little French and less English but communicates everything he needs to through the medium of mild disappointment. He can hear a problem in an engine from thirty metres. He has opinions about Italian food in Paris that he will express only through small, sorrowful sighs.

---

### THE GERMAN TEAM — *Präzision*
*Methodical, prepared, dangerous in a completely different way*

The Germans arrived a week early and drove every route three times. They have folders. They have contingency plans for their contingency plans. Wilhelm Stahl has done this before — though the circumstances were different, and he does not discuss them. The team operates with the quiet efficiency of a machine that has been correctly calibrated, and they are not remotely interested in being liked.

**Tier 2. Hold: Stuttgart (home base). Reputation: Disciplined, Humourless, Thorough.**

**Disposition toward the English team:** Professional suspicion. They have assessed the crew already.

**Resources:** Comprehensive mechanical preparation. A network of German business contacts in Paris with wartime roots that Stahl occasionally reaches out to quietly. Contingency vehicles.

---

**Wilhelm Stahl** — *Cold, Patient, Ruthless*
White hair, pale eyes, immaculate driving gloves even off the track. Stahl is old enough to have driven staff cars across occupied France and smart enough never to mention it. He drives the Mercedes 300SL with the economy of movement of someone who wastes nothing — not fuel, not time, not people who are no longer useful. He has been watching everyone since he arrived. He will act only when he is certain. He is usually certain.

**Bruno Mahler** — *Precise, Ambitious, Brittle*
Trim beard, black turtleneck, never without a clipboard and a stopwatch. Bruno is thirty-two, too young for the war, old enough to have grown up in its shadow. He idolises Stahl in the way that people idolise things that frighten them slightly. His Porsche 911 is currently sidelined with a suspension fault he refuses to admit is beyond him. He is holding himself together through sheer methodology and it is starting to show hairline cracks.

**"Luki" Breuer** — *Eager, Loose-mouthed, Doomed*
Young, blonde, twitchy energy that hasn't found anywhere to go yet. Luki hero-worships everyone on the team and talks too much in four languages. He is genuinely fast and has no idea how good he could be, because he can't stop looking at what everyone else is doing. He has been drinking since the Roi de Paris briefing. At the afterparty at the Moulin Rouge he will say, loudly, that he knows what the whole thing is *really* about, and that he read something in a letter of Stahl's, and that the Mona Lisa—

He will not finish the sentence. Stahl will make sure of it before midnight.

---

### THE AMERICAN TEAM — *The California Kings*
*Loud, fast, lucky, and completely impossible to dislike*

Nobody invited the Americans. They showed up anyway, in a convoy, with a cooler of beer and a hand-painted banner that reads KINGS OF THE ROAD in letters two feet high. They have no political connections, no preparation folders, no local knowledge whatsoever, and an absolute certainty that this will all work out fine. Somehow this is not entirely wrong. They drive like they're trying to win a bar bet, which is exactly what got them here.

**Tier 2. Hold: Los Angeles (home). Reputation: Unpredictable, Generous, Indestructible.**

**Disposition toward the English team:** Instant, uncomplicated friendliness. They'll share their beer before they know your name.

**Resources:** Brute horsepower. An inexhaustible supply of American cigarettes that open more doors in Paris than anyone expects. A contact at the US Embassy who owes Jack a favour and has stopped asking why.

---

**Jack "Throttle" Mercer** — *Weathered, Instinctive, Loyal*
Greasy hair, leather jacket gone soft with age, five o'clock shadow that never quite becomes a beard. Jack drives the Mustang the way other people breathe — without thinking about it, which is when he's at his most dangerous. He's been racing since he was seventeen on dry lakebeds outside Barstow. He doesn't strategise. He reads the road and reacts, and his reactions are about a half-second faster than anyone who hasn't seen him drive would believe possible.

**Cal "Ironhand" Delaney** — *Solid, Deliberate, Intimidating*
Ex-boxer, grey temples, buzz cut so precise it looks regulation. Cal's left hand was rebuilt after a fight in '59 and the fingers don't close quite right, which is how he got the name, and why he switched to driving. He handles the Dodge Charger like a man moving furniture — purposeful, unhurried, and completely immovable once he's decided where he's going. He speaks very little but when he does, people stop talking.

**Tommy "Wrench" O'Hara** — *Cheerful, Scattered, Brilliant*
Backwards cap, coveralls with the arms torn off, mirrored shades, always eating something. Tommy maintains all three vehicles out of the back of the El Camino, which he also drives, and which contains a quantity of tools, parts, and fast food wrappers that defies the vehicle's apparent interior volume. He can fix anything given thirty minutes and something to argue with. He argues with machines the way other people argue with family — loudly, affectionately, and with complete confidence that he'll win.

## THE SUPPORTING CAST

---

**Madame Yvette Toussaint — "Rouge" / "Red"**
*Resilient, Warm, Dangerous*
A tabac on the Rue de la Roquette, just east of Bastille. Red hair gone silver at the temples now, kept pinned under a silk scarf when she's working, loose when she isn't. Yvette ran courier routes for the Resistance through the 11th and 12th arrondissements and she never once got caught, which tells you something about how she thinks. She knows everyone who matters in the eastern neighbourhoods and she owes nothing to anyone, which is the most powerful position in Paris. She calls the Face player *mon petit* regardless of their age, size, or feelings about it. She has a shotgun under the counter and has used it twice. She mentions this to no one.

**Sylvie Marceau — The Singer**
*Luminous, Perceptive, Guarded*
A jazz bar on the Rue de la Huchette in the 5th, Tuesday through Saturday, two sets a night. Early twenties, voice like smoke and brass, the kind of face that makes people stop walking. Sylvie watches everything from the stage and remembers all of it. She grew up in the 13th, she has a degree in philosophy she never mentions, and she is considerably more politically engaged than she lets on. She has friends among the student protesters and uses the bar as a quiet message drop when she needs to. She is careful about who she trusts and has reasons for that.

**Dr. Hélène Voss — The Physicist**
*Exacting, Restless, Privately Funny*
A junior researcher at the ENS Physics department on the Rue Lhomond. Late thirties, wire-rimmed glasses, hair that lost its argument with her this morning. Hélène works on signals theory and cryptography and would be irritated to be described as someone who helps people with Enigma machines, but that is what happened and she was very good at it. She has absolutely no interest in illegal racing but a considerable interest in unsolved problems of any kind. If you present something to her as a puzzle she cannot help herself.

**Armand Petit — The Curator**
*Fastidious, Haunted, Discreet*
A mid-level curator in the antiquities and wartime acquisitions department at the Louvre. Sixties, immaculate suit, the careful posture of someone who has spent decades around things that must not be dropped. Armand knows exactly what came into the Louvre collection during the Occupation and what the paperwork said and what the paperwork didn't say. He has been quietly anxious about several items for twenty years. He will help if approached correctly — which means not in the museum, not by phone, and not before he's had time to convince himself he's doing the right thing.

**Maurice Garnier — The Butcher**
*Blunt, Shrewd, Sentimental*
A boucherie in Les Halles, near the Rue Rambuteau, open from five in the morning. Big man, bloodstained apron, hands like shovels. Maurice was the missing link in the wartime chain — he moved things and people between networks that didn't know about each other, and he did it by being someone nobody looked at twice. He has not talked about any of this in twenty years and he will not enjoy talking about it now. He will do it anyway, for old reasons, and he will be gruff about it the entire time. His shop is also where you go if you need something found in Les Halles at short notice, for a consideration. The Vélosolex chase happened on his doorstep and he is still annoyed.

---

**Les Moustiques — The Gang of Kids**
*Quick, Mercenary, Ungovernable*

A loose pack of twelve to eighteen boys and girls, ages roughly ten to sixteen, based out of the market district around Les Halles and the edges of the Marais. They run errands, carry messages, pick pockets, and know every passage, service alley, and locked gate in the central arrondissements. They move on Vélosolexes — the cheap, wheezing little moped-bicycle hybrids that are everywhere in Paris — and they are extraordinarily fast on them through tight spaces where cars cannot follow.

They have no formal leader. The oldest one present is in charge, until someone faster or louder arrives. They are available for hire by anyone who can pay in cash, cigarettes, or food, and their loyalty lasts precisely as long as the arrangement does. The French team has used them before. This does not make them reliable — it makes them experienced.

The GM should give two or three of them names and personalities that recur. Suggested: **Milo** (thirteen, serious, the one who actually delivers things), **Didi** (fifteen, girl, the fast one, the one who took the Enigma ticket), and **Le Gros** (sixteen, large, slow on the bike, does not let this bother him).

## THE BIG FACTIONS

---

### THE STUDENT MOVEMENT — *Le Mouvement du 22 Mars*
*Idealistic, Fractious, and Briefly Capable of Anything*

Named for the date in March when students occupied the administration building at Nanterre. By May they have spread to the Sorbonne, the streets, and the imagination of half of France. The movement has no single leader and no coherent programme, which is simultaneously its greatest strength and the reason it will not, in the end, win. It is powered by genuine fury at a society that feels airless and controlled, and it has turned the Latin Quarter into a running battle of paving stones and tear gas.

**Tier 1** (rising). **Hold:** The Latin Quarter, the Sorbonne, Nanterre. **Reputation:** Passionate, Disorganised, Inspiring.

**Disposition toward the crew:** Suspicious of outsiders with money and fast cars, but potentially sympathetic if the crew demonstrates the right politics or the right enemies. A Fighter or Fixer with working-class roots gets a warmer reception.

**Resources:** Numbers. Barricades. The moral high ground, for now. A printing press in the basement of the Sorbonne producing pamphlets around the clock. Friends in the press. The ability to block a street in under ten minutes.

**Key figure — Daniel "Danny" Lefèvre:** *Eloquent, Exhausted, Incandescent.* Twenty-two, speaks in complete paragraphs, hasn't slept properly in two weeks. He is the person the press photographs and the person the prefect is most afraid of. He can move a crowd. He also needs someone to get him a car occasionally and has no particular objection to the crew's activities as long as they don't actively serve the state.

---

### THE UNIONS — *La CGT (Confédération Générale du Travail)*
*Powerful, Cautious, and Watching the Students Very Carefully*

The Confédération Générale du Travail is the largest trade union federation in France, communist-aligned, and deeply ambivalent about the student uprising. The workers and the students want different things and the CGT leadership knows it. But by mid-May the rank and file are moving anyway — wildcat strikes spreading from the factories into the transport system, the post, the utilities. Paris is beginning to seize up. The CGT didn't start this. They are now trying to decide whether to ride it or brake it.

**Tier 3. Hold:** The industrial suburbs, the factory belts, the transport networks. **Reputation:** Organised, Cautious, Formidable.

**Disposition toward the crew:** Neutral at best. They have no interest in racing. If the crew interferes with union action they will have problems. If the crew is useful to the rank and file in some concrete way, doors open.

**Resources:** The ability to shut down fuel supplies, transport links, and communication networks. Significant funds. A legal team. Contacts in every nationalised industry in France.

**Key figure — Henri Boulard:** *Careful, Powerful, Immovable.* Fifties, heavy, a shop steward for twenty years before he rose. He thinks in terms of decades and he does not trust anything that moves this fast. He is meeting with the government today. He will be meeting with them again tomorrow. He will not be rushed.

---

### LA GENDARMERIE NATIONALE
*Professional, Hierarchical, and Stretched Very Thin*

The Gendarmerie Nationale is the national military police force of France — gendarmes are technically soldiers, under the authority of the Ministry of Armed Forces, responsible for public order outside the major cities and for specialist functions within them. In May 1968 they are being pulled in every direction at once: student protests, union strikes, political pressure from the Élysée, and now apparently an illegal street race involving foreign nationals and a suspicious number of Italians.

**Tier 3. Hold:** National, with a commissariat relevant to the crew in the eastern suburbs. **Reputation:** By-the-book, slow to move, hard to stop once moving.

**Disposition toward the crew:** Professionally suspicious. They will add Heat ticks methodically and without drama. They do not improvise.

**Resources:** Jurisdiction, patience, and paperwork. A detective named **Inspecteur Moreau** (careful, thorough, mild) who has already opened a file on the foreign racing teams. He has not yet connected the cars to anything else. He will.

---

### LA CRS — *Compagnies Républicaines de Sécurité*
*(Republican Security Companies — the national riot police, under the Ministry of the Interior)*

Where the Gendarmerie is the slow machinery of the state, the CRS is its fist. The Compagnies Républicaines de Sécurité are France's dedicated public order police — mobile, paramilitary, trained specifically for crowd control and civil unrest. In May 1968 they are in their element and they know it. They move in formation, they use tear gas freely, and they have been fighting students in the Latin Quarter for two weeks. They are tired, angry, and not making fine distinctions.

**Tier 4. Hold:** Wherever the government points them. Currently: the Left Bank, the major boulevards, the approaches to government buildings. **Reputation:** Brutal, Effective, Indiscriminate.

**Disposition toward the crew:** The CRS do not care about illegal racing. They care about public order. If the crew drives through a protest, causes a scene near a barricade, or becomes associated with the disorder in any way, the CRS will redirect attention toward them with the full weight of a baton charge. They do not investigate — they contain.

**Resources:** Numbers, vehicles, tear gas, and the legal authority to detain anyone obstructing public order. A colonel named **Vautrin** (broad, red-faced, twenty years of this) who operates on the principle that if something is moving when it shouldn't be, it should be stopped.
