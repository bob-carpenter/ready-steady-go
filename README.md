# READY, STEADY, GO!
### A Forged in the Dark Game of Fast Cars, Faster Fists, and Faster Talk
#### Paris, May 1968

#### Bob Carpenter (with help from Claude)

---

The streets are burning. Students are throwing paving stones at riot police in the Latin Quarter.

De Gaulle is nervous. And somewhere in this chaos, the English team has rolled into Bagnolet in a transit van towing their best car, looking for glory — and whatever else falls their way.

The **Roi de Paris** illegal circuit race is the prize everyone can see. What nobody knows yet is that the real prize has been hidden in this city since the war.

---

## THE PLAYBOOKS

Choose one of four **playbooks**. Each gives you a starting Action and a special armor.

---

### DRIVER
*"I'll have us out of here before they know we've gone."*

You get **MANOEUVRE** as your starting Action.

Your special armor protects you against being caught, cornered, or run off the road when you're behind the wheel.

**Choose one special ability:**

- **FLAT OUT** — Mark 1–2 stress to push a vehicle beyond its limits: outrun anything, thread any gap, hold any line. Each stress you mark improves your effect by one level.
- **HEEL AND TOE** — You always act first in a chase or race, and you can react to sudden road hazards without rolling.
- **SLIPSTREAM** — When you set up another driver by getting ahead of them and creating an opening, they get +1 effect on their following roll.
- **COOL HEAD** — You ignore the first consequence of a crash or collision each scene.

---

### MECHANIC
*"Give me twenty minutes and a decent socket set."*

You get **TINKER** as your starting Action.

Your special armor protects you against equipment failure, sabotage, and anything going wrong with a vehicle or machine under your care.

**Choose one special ability:**

- **JURY RIG** — You can tick a segment on a repair or prep clock with **limited** effect just by looking at something and describing what you'd do. Doing it for real takes time and tools.
- **BOOSTED** — When you spend downtime prepping a vehicle, mark 1–4 stress during that session to give it +1 effect on any one Action the team names in advance (speed, handling, durability, disguise, etc.).
- **STRIPPED DOWN** — You can declare that a vehicle has a hidden modification — a concealed compartment, a ram bar, a second fuel tank — once per session, after the fact.
- **SPANNER** — +1d when fighting with improvised weapons or tools, or when breaking something on purpose.

---

### FIGHTER
*"You want to do this the easy way or the painful way?"*

You get **SKIRMISH** as your starting Action.

Your special armor protects you against physical harm in a brawl, fight, or standoff.

**Choose one special ability:**

- **OVERWHELMING** — Mark 1–4 stress to add that many dice when using violence or the threat of it. Each stress mark adds +1d.
- **HARD TARGET** — When someone attacks you, you may spend your special armor to force them to reroll and take the lower result.
- **INTIMIDATE** — +1 effect when threatening someone with credible violence, even if you don't follow through.
- **BRAWLER** — You can take harm in a fight on behalf of a teammate without rolling; they suffer no consequence and you take the hit.

---

### FIXER
*"Leave the talking to me."*

You get **CONSORT** as your starting Action.

Your special armor protects you against being compromised, pressured, or caught in a lie.

**Choose one special ability:**

- **SMOOTH** — When you talk your way out of trouble, everyone who was suspicious of you forgets their suspicion until they have specific new evidence.
- **WELL CONNECTED** — Once per session, you may declare that you know someone useful in the current situation. They owe you, or you have something on them.
- **READS THE ROOM** — You always know when someone is lying to you or hiding something. You may ask the GM one question about their true intentions and get a straight answer.
- **CHARMING** — +1d when dealing with someone you've previously done a favour for, bought a drink for, or otherwise made feel good about you.

## ATTRIBUTES & ACTIONS

You have three Attributes. Each starts at 0, and you choose one to set at 1 during character creation.

| Attribute | Actions |
|-----------|---------|
| **NERVE** | MANOEUVRE, SKIRMISH, WRECK |
| **CUNNING** | HUNT, SURVEY, TINKER |
| **PRESENCE** | CONSORT, SWAY, COMMAND |

**MANOEUVRE** — Drive, race, chase, evade, stunt, navigate.
**SKIRMISH** — Fight in close quarters, brawl, grapple, disarm.
**WRECK** — Destroy, ram, crash deliberately, cause mayhem.
**HUNT** — Track, tail, gather intelligence, spot a trap.
**SURVEY** — Case a location, read a situation, spot details others miss.
**TINKER** — Fix, modify, build, sabotage, pick locks, crack safes.
**CONSORT** — Work your contacts, trade favours, operate in the underworld.
**SWAY** — Persuade, deceive, seduce, bluff, talk your way in or out.
**COMMAND** — Direct, intimidate, rally, organise, hold a situation.

## CHARACTER CREATION

1. **Choose a playbook.** Note your starting Action and your special armor.
2. **Choose a special ability** from your playbook list.
3. **Choose an Attribute** and set it to 1. (The other two stay at 0.)
4. **Choose one additional Action** beyond the one granted by your playbook.
5. **Name your character.** Give them a look, a background, and a reason to be in Paris.

## ROLLING THE DICE

When you take an **Action**, build your dice pool:

- Start with dice equal to your **Attribute** rating.
- Add **+1d** if you have the matching **Action**.
- Add **+1d** for relevant bonuses (gear, position, assistance).

Pick the **highest single die** from your pool.

If your pool is **0 dice**, roll **2d** and take the **lowest**.

**Results:**
- **Double 6 (CRIT):** Success with increased effect.
- **6:** Success. You do it.
- **4–5:** Partial success. You do it, but suffer a consequence.
- **1–3:** Failure. Things go badly. You don't do it, and suffer a consequence.

### Position & Effect

Before you roll, the GM tells you your **position** (controlled, risky, or desperate) and your **effect level** (great, standard, or limited). The worse your position, the harsher the consequences. Effect level determines how much you accomplish on a success.

### The Devil's Bargain

You may ask the GM for a **Devil's Bargain** — a negative condition that will happen regardless of your roll's outcome — in exchange for **+1d**. Other players may also suggest bargains.

### Fortune Rolls

When the GM wants to determine an uncertain outcome without a player action, they build a dice pool from relevant traits (quality, magnitude, tier, etc.) and roll. A CRIT means exceptional results; 6 is a full success; 4–5 is partial; 1–3 is a bad result.

## STRESS & PUSHING

You have **9 stress boxes**.

When you suffer a consequence from a **partial success or failure**, you may **resist** it:

- **Controlled position:** costs **1 stress**
- **Risky position:** costs **2 stress**
- **Desperate position:** costs **3 stress**

You may also **Push Yourself** by spending **2 stress** to gain **+1d** on your roll, or to attempt something at the edge of human ability.

When you fill your **last stress box**, mark a **Trauma** and reset stress to 0. At **4 Trauma**, your character can't keep up the pace and retires.

---

## HARM & RESISTANCE

The GM may tell you to take **Harm** as a consequence. Harm is tracked in three levels:

- **Lesser harm** (-1 effect): a sprain, a cut, rattled nerves, a dented panel.
- **Serious harm** (-1d): a broken hand, bad concussion, a wheel torn off.
- **Severe harm** (disabled): you cannot take any Action unassisted without marking 2 stress.

If a harm box is already filled, tick the box to its left. If all your harm boxes are filled, your character is out — badly hurt, arrested, or worse.

### Resisting Harm

You can reduce incoming harm by **resisting** it — paying stress equal to the harm's severity:

- Reduce a **level 1 harm** to nothing: **1 stress**.
- Reduce a **level 2 harm** to nothing: **2 stress** (or to a level 1 harm for **1 stress**).
- Reduce a **level 3 harm** to nothing: **3 stress** (or step it down for 1 or 2 stress).

### Armor

If you're wearing or have declared relevant protective gear, tick your **armor box** instead of taking physical harm. Each playbook has a **special armor** for non-physical consequences.

## TEAMWORK

When the crew works together on the same goal, players may **lead a group action**.

- Each player rolls the **same Action**.
- **Discard the highest result. Take the second-highest.**
- If only one player succeeds, the action succeeds but at **reduced effect** — it lacks the scale and coordination a true group effort would have.
- The leader marks **1 stress** for every teammate who fails their roll.

This reflects the truth of group work: it's only as strong as the weakest link in the chain, and the best performer in the group is covering for someone.

**Assist:** You may spend **1 stress** to give a teammate **+1d** on their roll.

**Set Up:** Roll an Action to improve a teammate's position or increase the effect of their following roll.

**Protect:** You may step in to resist a consequence on a teammate's behalf, taking the stress yourself.

## VEHICLES & THE GARAGE

The crew shares a **garage in Bagnolet**, on the eastern edge of Paris. This is your base, your workshop, and the only place in the city that's really yours.

Vehicles are crew equipment. They enable things in the fiction — what you can attempt depends on what you have available. The GM and players together establish what's in the garage at the start of play. Standard options include:

- **Sports cars** — fast, low, conspicuous, fragile.
- **Sedans** — fast enough, anonymous, good for surveillance or blending in.
- **Utility vehicles & vans** — slow, capacious, can carry people or things that shouldn't be seen.
- **Trucks** — muscle, intimidation, useful for blocking roads or shifting heavy cargo.
- **Motorcycles** — quick through traffic, no cover, high risk.

### Load

Before a score or race, choose your **load**: light (quick, inconspicuous), normal (looks like you mean business), or heavy (packed, slow, drawing attention).

During the action, check off items as you need them, up to your load maximum.

## RUNNING A RACE

Races don't use a separate structured mechanic — they play out through Action rolls, fiction, and consequences. But here's how to think about them:

**Set up the clock.** A race is a **progress clock** with 4–8 segments depending on length and complexity. Each team or driver works against the same clock, or against rival clocks.

**Position reflects your standing.** A driver who's been cut off, boxed in, or forced wide is in a **risky** or **desperate** position. A driver who's made a clean overtake is in **controlled**.

**Effect reflects your pace.** A tuned-up, prepped car in clear air gets **great** effect. A van on a circuit, or a car with a damaged tyre, gets **limited** effect.

**Consequences in a race:**
- Losing ground (a rival ticks their clock further)
- Mechanical damage (a harm box on the car, fiction-level only, handled by the GM)
- Physical harm (if it goes badly enough)
- Attracting attention (tick the Heat clock)

**The teams race too.** Rival drivers use Fortune rolls when the players aren't directly engaging with them. Roll their relevant quality and apply the result to their clock.

**Example — the Palmarium to the Arc de Triomphe, 3 laps and back:**
Set this as an 8-segment clock. Great effect = 3 segments, standard = 2, limited = 1. Each roll represents a meaningful phase of the race (the charge out of the Bois, fighting through traffic on the Avenue Foch, the tight turn at the Arc itself). Rivals roll Fortune at each phase unless a player does something to directly interfere with them.

## HEAT & THE GENDARMERIE

The crew has a **Heat clock** with 8 segments. As the crew draws police attention — through noise, violence, high-profile stunts, or bad luck — the GM ticks the clock.

**Tick Heat when:**
- A score or race was loud, chaotic, or very public (+1)
- Someone was seriously hurt or killed (+2)
- A rival tips off the authorities
- The crew is spotted and described

**When the Heat clock fills**, the Gendarmerie or CRS have enough to move. Expect a cordon, a raid on the garage, or a roadblock. This doesn't mean it's over — it means the situation has escalated dramatically and the crew needs to act accordingly.

The Heat clock resets (partially or fully) if the crew lies low, bribes the right people, or the student uprising helpfully draws all available officers away.

---

## THE OPPOSITION

Four teams are competing for the **Roi de Paris** — and for whatever else is worth having in this city. Each team has principals (the drivers and principals) and support (mechanics, fixers, spotters). They function like rival factions: they have interests, resources, and a disposition toward the English team.

The GM builds and tracks the teams between sessions. How teams react to the crew's actions — with retaliation, negotiation, an approach, or sabotage — is the ongoing texture of the campaign.

## DOWNTIME

After each score or major event, deal with any loose ends. Then take **two downtime actions**:

- **Clear one Harm box** (from right to left).
- **Indulge your vice** to clear **3 stress boxes**.
- **Tick a segment** on a long-term project clock (repairs, research, contacts, planning).
- **Gather information** about a target, person, or location.
- **Work on the car** — a Mechanic may spend a downtime action to prep a vehicle for the next job.

---

## COIN & STASH

After a successful score, the crew earns **4–12 coin** to split. Coin represents a few hundred pounds sterling or its equivalent. You can spend 1 coin to buy an extra downtime action, or bank it.

A better stash means a better lifestyle and a more comfortable retirement. **1–4 stash:** roughing it. **5–8 stash:** comfortable. **9–12 stash:** living well.

---

## SESSION END: ADVANCEMENT

Mark XP when:
- You roll a **desperate action**.
- At session's end, mark 1 XP for each:
  - You expressed your character's personality, background, or personal stakes.
  - You struggled with your vices or past.
  - The team together expressed their collective drive, conflict, or style.
  - You addressed a challenge with your playbook's approach:
    - **Driver:** by outdriving someone or outrunning a situation.
    - **Mechanic:** by fixing, modifying, or building something that mattered.
    - **Fighter:** by direct physical confrontation or credible threat of it.
    - **Fixer:** by charming, bribing, deceiving, or talking your way through.

**When you fill your XP track**, take one benefit:
- Raise an Attribute from 0 to 1.
- Raise an Attribute from 1 to 2.
- Mark a new Action.
